# RadRoot talk track: Start with their why

About 6 minutes spoken. Lines in [brackets] are cues for you, not words to say. Use this in HR rooms, where the founding seats are the one offer.

## 1. The hook (30 seconds)

Quick show of hands. Who here has read Start With Why, or seen the Simon Sinek talk?

[Pause for hands.]

Okay. Most of us. And it worked. Simon Sinek told us to start with why, and we listened. Most companies have a why now. It's on the website. It's on the wall by the coffee machine. It's slide two of the onboarding deck.

But here's the thing. It's our why.

## 2. The turn (1 minute)

I tell people that knowing your own why is the easy part. The hard part, the part that actually lets you serve people better, is knowing theirs.

Why did that candidate turn down the offer? Why did that client renew, or walk? Why is your best manager quietly polishing up their LinkedIn profile?

Employees. Clients. Candidates. Contractors. Every one of them has a why. And their why is the thing that tells you what to do next.

So how do we find it out today?

## 3. The tools we have (1 minute)

Two options, more or less.

Option one: we talk to them. Stay interviews, exit interviews, a cup of coffee. Great answers. Real stories. And it does not scale. You can't sit down with 500 people. Well, you can. But then that's your whole quarter.

Option two: we send a survey. Scales beautifully. Everybody gets it. And what comes back? A 3.8 out of 5. And, most of the time, an empty comment box.

So one gets the why and doesn't scale. The other scales and rarely gets the why.

## 4. The translation problem (1.5 minutes)

Here's why the survey misses. Think about what we're asking people to do.

Somebody has a story in their head. Something like, "My manager is great, but nobody told me this job was going to be this much travel."

Then we hand them a scale and say, turn that into a number. So they do. They pick a 3. That's translation number one.

Then that 3 lands on somebody's desk. And now that person has to turn it back into a story. Why a 3? Was it the manager? The pay? The travel? They guess. That's translation number two.

Two translations. Something gets lost every time. It's a game of telephone where one of the players is a spreadsheet.

By the time it gets back to you, it isn't their story anymore. It's your best guess about their story.

Bill Murray starred in a whole movie about this. Lost in Translation. Great movie. Terrible feedback strategy.

## 5. What RadRoot does (1.5 minutes)

So I built RadRoot to take the translating out.

Instead of a scale, RadRoot runs a short interview. Five questions by default, about two minutes. Give it your list, and each question is written for that person, from what you already know about them. And each one follows the answer they just gave. Mark something low, and the next question digs into that. Not into the parking lot.

They tap an answer. If none of them fits, they tap Other and type their own. No login. Nothing to download.

What comes back isn't a number you have to decode. It's their story, written out in plain sentences from their own answers. And you can read every answer behind it.

Story in. Story out. No number in the middle for anybody to guess at.
